# Four explicit changes to the prior provisional contract

## 1. D uptake now uses Michaelis–Menten kinetics

For science-labelled P12 and P15 runs, the earlier linear assumption

[
J_D=k_{u,D}C_D
]

is superseded by an inward-only Michaelis–Menten surface flux:

[
J_{u,i}
=======

J_{\max}
\frac{\max(C^m_{D,i},0)}
{K_m+\max(C^m_{D,i},0)}
\qquad
\left[
\mathrm{mol\ P,cm^{-2},s^{-1}}
\right],
]

[
\Delta N^{\mathrm{req}}_{u,i}
=============================

A^u_iJ_{u,i}\Delta t.
]

There is no `0.5` prefactor, no reverse term, and no D efflux in v1.

This is a substantive improvement over the prior assumption because the Bisot study directly formulates P uptake as a surface-area-scaled Michaelis–Menten process and reports an approximately constant saturated surface flux.  The supplement gives the corresponding low-P reaction–diffusion form and parameter table.  

The provisional imported values are:

[
J_{\max}
========

2.95949412514\times10^{-12}
\ \mathrm{mol\ P,cm^{-2},s^{-1}},
]

corresponding to (3.3\ \mathrm{ng\ P,mm^{-2},h^{-1}}), and

[
K_m
===

1.00084710414\times10^{-9}
\ \mathrm{mol\ P,cm^{-3}},
]

corresponding to (3.1\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{\mu g\ P,mm^{-3}}).

These are **literature-linked priors, not BMX-calibrated coefficients**.

At the two approved environmental-D concentrations, the Michaelis–Menten saturation fractions are approximately:

[
\frac{C_{\mathrm{low}}}{K_m+C_{\mathrm{low}}}=0.9415,
\qquad
\frac{C_{\mathrm{high}}}{K_m+C_{\mathrm{high}}}=0.9783.
]

Thus, both initial environments are close to transporter saturation. This implies that the low/high contrast may emerge primarily through depletion and replenishment dynamics rather than a simple 2.8-fold initial uptake-rate difference. That is an inference from the selected law and published parameter values.

The old linear `k_u,D` law remains available only as:

```text
ENGINEERING_REGRESSION_COMPARATOR
```

It may not be used for the claim-bearing P12 or P15 result.

# Prior P10 and P11 answers retained

## P10 topology

The earlier topology decisions remain effective:

* Raw particle deletion without a complete successor map is prohibited and hard-aborts before state disappears.
* Split and fusion events transfer D, E, and F amounts exactly once to valid successors.
* Orphan bonds are deterministically remapped when a unique valid successor exists.
* A material-bearing or unresolved orphan bond hard-aborts; its material is never silently exported, destroyed, or assigned to a convenient neighbor.
* Disjoint simultaneous topology events use a common pre-event snapshot and exactly-once ownership.
* Overlapping or conflicting simultaneous events cause an atomic abort before any member of the event batch commits.
* Out-of-domain deletion is prohibited.

## P11 geometry and outer boundaries

The earlier geometry decisions remain effective:

* Solid divider and frame surfaces use hard nonpenetration.
* Outward normal motion is removed while tangential motion is preserved.
* The aperture interior remains mechanically open.
* Elastic reflection, penalty-force repulsion, and particle deletion are not used.
* Outer x and z mesh boundaries are homogeneous no-flux for D and inactive F.
* Outer x and z particle boundaries use hard nonpenetration with tangential sliding.
* A fixed-concentration x-boundary may be used only as an explicitly identified, separately ledgered sensitivity.

## P16-U10 — aggregation and dominance

For each configuration:

* Display every replicate.
* Report median, IQR, and min–max.
* At (n\ge10), use 10,000 paired block-bootstrap resamples for 95% intervals.
* At (n=5), report screening summaries only.
* Use configuration medians for dominance, not individual runs.
* Report point frontier, robust frontier, and uncertain-membership set.
* Perform leave-one-block-out frontier stability.

Point dominance for maximize objectives:

[
\widetilde y_{a,k}
\ge
\widetilde y_{b,k}-\epsilon_k
\quad\forall k,
]

with:

[
\widetilde y_{a,k}

>

\widetilde y_{b,k}+\epsilon_k
]

for at least one (k).

Robust dominance requires the lower 95% bound for (a) to be at least the upper 95% bound for (b), within (\epsilon_k), on every objective and clearly above it on at least one.

No single winner may be selected. The proposal similarly treats a Pareto point only as nondominated among the tested configurations, not as biologically optimal.
