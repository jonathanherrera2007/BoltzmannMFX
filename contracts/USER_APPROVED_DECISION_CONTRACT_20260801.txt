# Recommended BMX phosphorus decision contract

The answers below are the decisions I recommend adopting for the bounded, exploratory, uncalibrated v1 model. They are sufficiently explicit for implementation and preregistration, but they do **not** constitute prior mentor approval: the proposal packet itself states that its recommendations remain unapproved until reviewed.

# 1. P10 topology-transfer semantics

## 1.1 Governing invariant

A topology operation is **not** a phosphorus source or sink. Every pre-event mole of D, E, and F must have exactly one of these outcomes before the original owner disappears:

1. It is assigned exactly once to a live successor particle.
2. It is credited exactly once to an already-approved cumulative destination such as `structural_p` or `exported_p`.
3. The update hard-aborts before any state is destroyed.

For v1, `other_exit_d`, `other_exit_e`, `other_exit_f`, and `other_exit_events` should remain zero. No lysis, absorbing boundary, unexplained cleanup loss, or generic particle-death pathway is authorized.

## 1.2 Exact event rules

| Event                                                            | Classification                | Required behavior                                                                                                         | Reason code                                | Conservation destination                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| Parent disappears because of split                               | Permitted topology transfer   | Capture D/E/F amounts before the split; partition each amount by resulting child volume; commit once                      | `P10_SPLIT_PARENT_REPLACED`                | Successor-child internal D/E/F                |
| Predecessor disappears because of an approved fusion/replacement | Permitted topology transfer   | Add participating amounts before recomputing concentrations in the unique successor                                       | `P10_FUSION_PREDECESSOR_REPLACED`          | Successor internal D/E/F                      |
| Raw particle deletion without a complete successor map           | Prohibited; hard abort        | Abort before marking, deleting, compacting, or redistributing the particle                                                | `P10_ABORT_UNMAPPED_PARTICLE_DELETION`     | None; no ledger mutation                      |
| Out-of-domain particle removal                                   | Prohibited; hard abort        | Do not delete it. Apply the boundary rule; if a valid in-domain state cannot be constructed, abort                        | `P10_ABORT_OUT_OF_DOMAIN_PARTICLE`         | None; no ledger mutation                      |
| Bond endpoint replaced by topology                               | Permitted remap               | Resolve each old endpoint through the frozen predecessor-to-successor map                                                 | `P10_BOND_ENDPOINT_REMAPPED`               | No P transfer; bond is not an inventory owner |
| Redundant zero-state self-loop or duplicate bond after remapping | Permitted zero-amount cleanup | Canonically prune the redundant relationship                                                                              | `P10_ZERO_STATE_BOND_PRUNED`               | `NONE_ZERO_AMOUNT`                            |
| Bond has no unique live successor endpoint                       | Hard abort                    | Abort the entire topology stage before cleanup                                                                            | `P10_ABORT_ORPHAN_BOND_NO_SUCCESSOR`       | None                                          |
| Orphaned bond has nonzero pending material/transfer state        | Hard abort                    | Abort; do not export or assign the amount arbitrarily                                                                     | `P10_ABORT_ORPHAN_BOND_PENDING_MATERIAL`   | None                                          |
| Disjoint simultaneous topology events                            | Permitted exactly-once batch  | Evaluate from one pre-event snapshot; each predecessor participates in at most one event and each amount is assigned once | `P10_SIMULTANEOUS_DISJOINT_BATCH`          | Corresponding successors                      |
| Simultaneous events share a particle, bond, or destination       | Rejected; hard abort          | Reject the entire batch before any topology mutation                                                                      | `P10_ABORT_SIMULTANEOUS_TOPOLOGY_CONFLICT` | None                                          |

## 1.3 Simultaneous-event ownership rule

Use an **exactly-once multi-event rule**, not outcome-dependent serialization:

1. Capture a single immutable pre-topology snapshot.
2. Generate a canonical event key such as:

```text
(event_type, minimum_global_particle_id, maximum_global_particle_id, target_id)
```

3. Build a conflict graph in which events are connected when they share a particle, bond, or proposed successor.
4. Events in singleton/disjoint components may be executed in canonical order for byte-level determinism, but their scientific semantics remain snapshot-based.
5. Any connected component containing more than one competing event causes an atomic hard abort of the complete topology stage.
6. No partial subset may commit before conflict detection finishes.

This avoids both duplicate ownership and arbitrary order-dependent biology.

## 1.4 Orphaned-bond material

**Material attached to an orphaned bond does not leave the system and is not transferred to a convenient neighboring particle.**

The v1 representation should treat D/E/F as particle-owned, not bond-owned. Any bond-local quantity used during transport must be fully committed or zero before topology cleanup begins. Therefore:

* A topological bond relationship may be remapped or pruned.
* A bond may not carry persistent unaccounted P.
* Detection of nonzero bond-local material during orphan cleanup is a hard failure.
* No `other_exit_*` ledger is credited.

## 1.5 Required event record before disappearance

Every permitted predecessor replacement must write, before destruction:

```text
event_id
global_step
simulation_time
reason_code
event_type
ordered_predecessor_ids
ordered_successor_ids
predecessor_D/E/F_amounts
successor_D/E/F_amounts
ledger_destination
effective_configuration_hash
pre_topology_hash
post_topology_hash
local_conservation_residual
MPI_owner_before
MPI_owner_after
```

The event record and material transfer must be one atomic transaction. A state may not disappear first and be ledgered afterward.

## 1.6 Already-approved non-topology destinations

Two apparent “disappearances” are already accounted transformations rather than particle deletion:

* Accepted E used for realized growth goes to `structural_p`, reason code `P13_ACCEPTED_GROWTH_STRUCTURALIZATION`.
* Accepted D export goes to `exported_p`, reason code `P14_INTERFACE_EXPORT_ACCEPTED`.

Neither authorizes deleting the particle itself.

# 2. D membrane-uptake coefficient and law

## Decision

Do **not** use the reversible carbon equation as the primary D uptake law. In particular, do not copy either the reverse term or the unexplained `0.5` prefactor.

Use inward-only, surface-area-scaled uptake:

[
J_{u,i}
=======

k_{u,D}\max(C^m_{D,i},0),
]

where

[
J_{u,i}\quad [\mathrm{mol\ P,cm^{-2},s^{-1}}],
]

and

[
\Delta N^{\mathrm{req}}_{u,i}
=============================

A^u_i,J_{u,i},\Delta t.
]

Here:

* (C^m_{D,i}) is interpolated mesh-D concentration in (\mathrm{mol/cm^3}).
* (A_i^u) is the eligible hyphal area in (\mathrm{cm^2}), counted exactly once.
* (k_{u,D}) has units (\mathrm{cm/s}).
* The outward coefficient is exactly zero in v1.

The proposed exploratory range is:

[
k_{u,D}\in
{2\times10^{-6},\ 2\times10^{-5},\ 2\times10^{-4}}
\ \mathrm{cm/s},
]

with

[
k_{u,D}^{\mathrm{centre}}
=========================

2\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{cm/s}.
]

This centre is a BMX-continuity/engineering value, not a measured whole-hypha phosphate uptake coefficient. 

## Shared-donor cap

When several segments draw from the same represented mesh donor cell (j), calculate every request before committing any transfer:

[
R_j=\sum_{i\in j}\Delta N^{\mathrm{req}}_{u,i},
]

[
s_j
===

\begin{cases}
\min\left(1,\dfrac{\max(N^m_{D,j},0)}{R_j}\right), & R_j>0,[6pt]
0, & R_j=0,
\end{cases}
]

[
\Delta N^{\mathrm{acc}}_{u,i}
=============================

s_j\Delta N^{\mathrm{req}}_{u,i}.
]

Then atomically debit mesh D and credit internal D. This makes the result independent of particle iteration order and prevents donor overdraw. 

## Why the carbon form should not be copied

A reversible term would imply a D efflux mechanism that has not been selected or calibrated. A future reversible model may introduce an independently named (k_{out,D}), also in cm/s, but it should not silently inherit the carbon reverse coefficient. The proposal explicitly treats reversible linear exchange as a later alternative rather than the primary v1 law. 

**Answer to the “leave unselected” option:** the law, units, range, and engineering centre should be selected now; their biological interpretation remains unselected.

# 3. P export coefficient

## Operand and law

(k_{\mathrm{export}}) operates on local internal-D **amount**, in moles, not directly on concentration.

For segment (i), let (f_i^I\in[0,1]) be its geometrically eligible interface fraction and

[
N^I_{D,i}=f_i^I N_{D,i}.
]

Then:

[
\Delta N^{\mathrm{req}}_{P,i}
=============================

N^I_{D,i}
\left(1-e^{-k_{\mathrm{export}}\Delta t}\right),
]

[
\Delta N^{\mathrm{acc}}_{P,i}
=============================

\min\left(
\Delta N^{\mathrm{req}}*{P,i},
N*{D,i}
\right).
]

Therefore,

[
k_{\mathrm{export}}\quad [\mathrm{s^{-1}}].
]

Using an amount operand is important because it remains well defined through volume changes and segment refinement. The contact fraction supplies the local geometric scaling. 

## Values

Use:

[
k_{\mathrm{export}}
\in
{0,\ 10^{-6},\ 10^{-5},\ 10^{-4}}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}.
]

Interpretation:

* `0 s^-1`: mandatory no-export control and current claim-bearing central setting.
* `1e-6 s^-1`: slow exploratory export.
* `1e-5 s^-1`: provisional export-enabled engineering centre.
* `1e-4 s^-1`: fast exploratory sensitivity.

The nonzero values are broad model-only sensitivities, not empirical estimates. Nonzero export should remain off in science-labelled runs until the mentor explicitly authorizes the law and range. Synthetic and analytical operator tests may use `TEST_ONLY: 1e-5 s^-1`.  

Thus, the law and units should **not** remain unselected; only the choice of a nonzero biological/scientific default remains unselected.

## Fixed protocol

```text

0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 24, 36, 48 h

1.61e-8 and 4.52e-8 mol/cm3, separate co-primary cases
```

The endpoint is fixed. Do not stop when a chosen depletion percentage appears, and do not extend the run because depletion is absent. Machine timeout is operational failure, not a scientific stopping rule. 

## Required cases for each mesh-D arm

1. Active uptake with the full-surface rule.
2. (k_{u,D}=0).
3. Empty/no-network control.
4. Zero-mesh-D control.
5. Terminal-zone uptake sensitivity.

Yes: **one canonical deterministic run plus one exact clean-environment rerun is sufficient** for each approved case.

The two executions establish numerical reproducibility. They are not pooled or reported as (n=2), and they are not biological replicates.


Where software requires a seed field syntactically, use a fixed sentinel such as `0`, but assert that no random-number stream was initialized or consumed.

At every scheduled observation:

* Total mesh D and total internal D.

The primary P12 observables are mesh-D loss, internal-D gain, cumulative uptake, uptake per eligible area, near/far D, distance-binned qualitative depletion, conservation, and boundary sensitivity. 

## Grid and timestep

Do **not** select one physical resolution in the science decision. Permit a predeclared convergence study to select it.

Use at least three nested grid/timestep levels. The numerical contract must freeze:

Grid levels, timesteps, MPI ranks, and decompositions are numerical checks, not replicates.

# 8. P16 explorer/exploiter experiment

## 8.1 Current scientific status

Actual P16 execution remains:

```text
strategy_class           = UNCLASSIFIED_FOR_P16
P16_actual_science_status = BLOCKED_ON_EXTERNAL_STRATEGY_BASELINE
```

until the external strategy author's frozen C-only strategy contract is supplied and reproduced.

That dependency must contain the exact source, executable, strategy definitions, ordered configuration memberships, input files, objectives, normalization, replicate unit, seeds, raw outputs, scripts, expected outputs, Pareto result, and claim limitations. 

## 8.2 Precise semantic definitions

Subject to the external strategy author's actual frozen definitions:

* **Explorer:** a prospectively classified C-only strategy that allocates more strongly toward range expansion, with the hypothesis of higher front/wave speed and lower local saturation density.
* **Exploiter:** a prospectively classified C-only strategy that allocates more strongly toward local network densification/resource-extraction capacity, with the hypothesis of higher saturation density and lower range-expansion speed.

These are hypotheses associated with predeclared classes. Realized BMX outcomes may not assign or change the label.

The 2026 PNAS study describes the empirical framing as fast range expansion versus fast resource extraction and reports an exploration–exploitation Pareto front, but this does not supply an automatic configuration mapping into BMX. ([PubMed][1])

## 8.3 Use of (q_P) and (k_{gP})

**Prohibited for defining strategy membership.**

They may be used only as matched, within-strategy phosphorus sensitivities after explorer/exploiter labels have been frozen from the C-only authority.

Therefore:

```text
low q_P ≠ explorer by definition
high q_P ≠ exploiter by definition
high k_gP ≠ explorer or exploiter by definition
realized high uptake ≠ exploiter by definition
realized high speed ≠ explorer by definition
```

Outcome-based or parameter-based relabeling would be circular.

# 9. Geometry

## Particle-divider response

Select:

```text
hard nonpenetration / remove outward normal motion
```

For the **solid divider and frame**:

1. Detect the first physical contact of the finite-radius segment capsule.
2. Project the proposed point or segment to the nearest valid fungus-side position.
3. Remove the displacement/velocity component normal to the solid surface.
4. Preserve tangential movement.
5. Apply the constraint before geometry acceptance and before B/E debit.
6. Record attempted penetration, projected motion, tangential motion, and rejected growth.

Do not use elastic reflection, a penalty/repulsive force, or particle deletion. Elastic reflection introduces an arbitrary rebound; repulsive forces introduce stiffness and timestep dependence; deletion creates an unauthorized material exit. 

## Aperture behavior

The aperture interior is not a solid wall. A segment may pass through or occupy the aperture when its finite-radius geometry fits.

At aperture edges:

* solid-frame collision has precedence;
* contact/export eligibility is evaluated exactly once per segment per update;
* no Eulerian mesh-P flux is permitted through the aperture, consistent with the already-frozen divider rule.

Thus fungal mechanical access and Eulerian P permeability remain separate concepts.

## Outer x-boundary conditions

For mesh D and inactive mesh F:

[
\mathbf n\cdot(-D\nabla C)=0
]

at both (x_{\min}) and (x_{\max}) in the primary closed-domain experiment.

Particles use the same hard, nonpenetrating, tangentially sliding wall rule. No out-of-domain deletion is permitted.

## Outer z-boundary conditions

For mesh D and inactive mesh F:

[
\mathbf n\cdot(-D\nabla C)=0
]

at (z_{\min}) and (z_{\max}).

Particles again use hard nonpenetration with tangential motion retained. The y direction remains periodic.

The primary domain and sensitivities are:

```text
central x:       [-1, 1] cm
small x:         [-0.5, 0.5] cm
large x:         [-2, 2] cm
z:               [0, 1.2] cm
periodic y:      [0, 0.1) cm
```

A fixed-concentration reservoir at (x_{\max}) may be used only as an explicitly ledgered sensitivity, never as the primary closed-system boundary.

## Holdout design

Never split individual timepoints from the same plate between calibration and validation. The experimental unit must be the complete plate/network replicate.

Use two validation layers:

### A. In-distribution plate holdout

Within each sufficiently populated stratum defined by:

```text
fungal strain × root genotype × P treatment
```

hash-sort the complete plate IDs and prospectively assign:

```text
calibration/analysis development: 70%
qualification:                    15%
final sealed validation:          15%
```

All timepoints, images, spatial measurements, and derived values from one plate remain in the same partition.

### B. Condition-level extrapolative holdout

Before fitting, reserve one complete biological condition family—preferably:

* one root genotype, or
* one fungal strain with sufficient replicates, or
* one entire low-P treatment family—

that is not used to select coefficients, observation equations, normalization, thresholds, or claim language.

The exact family should be selected by a completeness audit and frozen before inspecting BMX agreement, not selected because it produces a favorable result.

## What may be validated

With an approved mapping and sufficient metadata, potential held-out comparisons include:

* wave/front speed;
* saturation length or surface-area density;
* network radius and expansion trajectories;
* spatially aggregated P extraction;
* total P transferred or depleted;
* qualitative depletion ordering across space;
* treatment-direction effects;
* root-genotype or fungal-strain contrasts.

Direct pointwise profile validation requires matching geometry, observation operator, spatial registration, sampling volume, and experimental uncertainty. It cannot be inferred merely from a visually similar field.

## Why RG-SCI remains `NO-GO`

The external data have already informed some contextual choices, such as plausible time horizons and qualitative trade-off framing. Therefore, those exact observations cannot automatically serve as untouched validation evidence.

`RG-SCI:GO` requires all of the following to be completed first:

1. Freeze exact dataset versions and hashes.
2. Inventory plate IDs, conditions, missingness, units, geometry, and measurement uncertainty.
3. Define the experimental-to-BMX observation operator.
4. Freeze calibration, qualification, and sealed validation partitions.
5. Ensure no held-out plate or condition influenced parameter selection.
6. Predeclare quantitative validation metrics and acceptance thresholds.
7. Validate numerical convergence independently of empirical agreement.
8. Execute the sealed holdout only once under the frozen contract.
9. Retain failed and null comparisons.
10. Obtain independent scientific review of the mapping and interpretation.

Until that occurs, the public datasets are **candidate validation resources**, not completed validation evidence. The appropriate status remains:

```text
RG-SW: eligible to proceed under the bounded exploratory contract
RG-SCI: NO-GO
```
